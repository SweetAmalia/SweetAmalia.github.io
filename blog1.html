How I Built My Portfolio

A simple project that respresents my skills, projects and personality as an IT student.

September 2026

Starting with the design

Before writing any code, I started by thinking about what I wanted the website to look and feel like. I created mockups of the different pages in Canva so I could experiment with the layout without having to worry about the technical side of the website yet.

One of my main goals was to create a design that feels professional while still showing some of my personality. I looked at different colour palettes and decided to use blue as one of the main colours. Blue gives the website a professional and familiar feeling, while also being a colour that I personally like. On top of my personal choice, I also checked the colours using a colour contrast analyser. This helped me make sure that the colours I chose were not only visually appealing, but also accessible and readable for visitors.

Inspired by LinkedIn and recipe websites

Two different ideas influenced the structure of my portfolio. For the personal part of the website, I liked the idea of a "who am I?" layout similar to the structure people are familiar with from LinkedIn. This makes information about me easy to find and gives recruiters a familiar way of navigating through my profile.

For my projects, I took inspiration from something completely different: recipe websites.

When looking at a recipe, you usually get a clear image, the required time, the difficulty level, and a short description before deciding whether you want to look at the full recipe. I wanted to apply a similar idea to my projects, Each project can show an image, a short explanation, and information about the project. This gives visitors a quick overview while still allowing them to explore the project in more detail if they are interested.

Designing for the people who visit

While designing the website, I also thought about who would actually be using it. One of my main user scenarios is a recruiter visiting my portfolio to look at my projects.

I wanted a recruiter to be able to find my projects quickly and understand what each project is about without having to search through large amounts of text. Because of this, the projects page uses a clear overview with an image, title, and short description for each project. I also included filtering options and a search bar. This gives visitors the option to browse through the projects or immediately look for something specific.

From mockup to code

Building the website myself also meant that I had to think about more than just how something looks. I had to consider the structure of the HTML, how the different sections would work together, how the layout would behave on different screen sizes, and how visitors would navigate through the website.

This process helped me understand how much planning can influence the development process. Having a clear idea of the structure beforehand made it easier to work on the code because I already had a direction to work towards.

Looking ahead

There is still plenty I would like to improve and add to my portfolio. As I continue learning, I want the website to develop alongside me and reflect the new skills and experiences I gain.

For now, this website represents where I am in my journey as an IT student. It combines the things I have learned with the work I have created, while giving me a place to show where I want to go next.
